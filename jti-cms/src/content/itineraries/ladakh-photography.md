    body: 'Distance / Time: 5 km / 15 minutes’ drive to hotel. Altitude: 3,500 meters (11,500 feet). Arrive at Kushok Bakula Rimpochee Airport. The entire morning and afternoon must be spent strictly resting at your hotel to acclimatize to the sudden high altitude. In the late evening, take a slow, gentle walk around the Leh Main Market. Leh Market is a historic trading hub of the old Silk Route.   - label: Day 2
    title: Leh local monasteries (110 km/4 to 5 hrs, approx.)
    body: Morning drive 45 km south to Hemis Monastery, the wealthiest and largest monastery in Ladakh. Hemis (Drukpa Lineage) houses a spectacular museum of ancient thangkas, gold statues, and sacred relics. It is famous for the Hemis Festival. Mid-day, on the way back, visit Stakna Monastery, perched dramatically on a tiger-nose-shaped rock in the middle of the Indus River. Stakna (Drukpa Lineage) offers a 360-degree panoramic view of the Indus Valley and houses a beautiful silver-gilded chorten. Afternoon drive to the north-west of Leh to visit Phyang Monastery. Phyang (Drikung Kagyu Lineage) is famous for its massive 900-year-old museum containing outdoor shrines, old Kashmiri bronzes, and Chinese silks. Evening is free for leisure. Overnight in Leh.

  - label: Day 3
    title: Leh – Alchi via Basgo, Likir & Saspol Cave (75 km/3 hrs, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, drive down the Srinagar-Leh highway. Stop at Basgo Monastery, a crumbling mud-brick fortress complex overlooking the village. The Basgo Fort is listed among the World Monuments Watch; it represents the old capital of lower Ladakh. The temples house a massive clay idol of Maitreya (Future Buddha). Continue to Likir Monastery, situated a short detour from the highway. Likir (Gelugpa Lineage) is renowned for its colossal 75-foot outdoor golden statue of Maitreya Buddha. Drive to Saspol Caves, located just off the road near the bridge. Saspol Caves are rare rock-cut cave temples dating back to the 13th-century, featuring stunning Buddhist wall paintings that fuse Tibetan and Indian art forms. Cross the river to reach the quiet village of Alchi. Overnight.

  - label: Day 4
    title: Alchi – Lamayuru (60 km/2 hrs, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, spend the morning exploring the Alchi Monastery Complex and enjoying a peaceful walk through the surrounding apricot orchards and traditional Ladakhi homes. Unlike other monasteries built on hills, Alchi sits on flat ground. It is one of the oldest preserved complexes in Ladakh (11th century). Its murals are global masterpieces, preserving a vibrant Kashmiri style of Buddhist art that has vanished elsewhere. In the afternoon, drive along the Indus River, ascending through the Khangral gorge towards Lamayuru. Overnight.

  - label: Day 5
    title: Lamayuru – Mulbekh & Kargil (105 km/4 hrs, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, explore Lamayuru Monastery and look down at the "Moonland" landscape. Perched precariously on a surreal, eroded clay landform resembling the moon. It is shrouded in myth as a place where a holy man dried up a deep lake. Mid-day, drive across the Fotu La Pass (the highest point on the Srinagar-Leh highway at 13,478 ft) down to Mulbekh to witness the ancient rock sculpture. Mulbekh Chamba is a magnificent, 9-meter-high rock-carved relief of Maitreya Buddha dating back to the Kushan or early medieval era (approx. 7th-8th century), standing right beside the highway. Later drive into Kargil town. Check-in at the hotel & overnight.

  - label: Day 6
    title: Kargil – Padum via Penzila Top (230 km/8 to 10 hrs approx.)
    body: An incredibly early start (4:00 AM – 5:00 AM) is necessary. Drive through the stunning, lush green Suru Valley, passing underneath the majestic twin glacier peaks of Nun (7135 m) and Kun (7087 m). As you pass through the Suru Valley, you will witness the unique culture of Balti-influenced Shia Muslims, which rapidly transforms into isolated, ancient Zanskari Buddhist culture once you cross the Penzi La pass. Cross the remote Penzi La Pass (4,400m), make a stop to witness the massive Drang Drung Glacier, and descend into the Zanskar Valley to reach Padum. Check-in at the hotel. Overnight.

  - label: Day 7
    title: Padum local sightseeing (40–50 km/2 hrs approx.)
    body: After breakfast, visit Sani Gompa, located flat on the valley floor. It is believed to be the oldest monastery in Zanskar, belonging to the Drukpa line. It contains the famous Kanika Chorten, traditionally linked to King Kanishka (2nd century CE). Drive 14 km out of Padum along the Stod River to visit the Tungri Nunnery (Samten Choling) – a rare, offbeat experience. It houses a small, self-sustaining community of Buddhist nuns (Chumos). The lifestyle here is simple, offering profound insights into female monasticism in the Himalayas Cross the valley to visit Karsha Monastery, the largest spiritual center in Zanskar. Karsha (Gelugpa Lineage) is aspectacular, sprawling white monastic complex tiered across a steep mountain cliff. It houses ancient avalokiteshvara shrines and offers unmatched views of the Padum valley floor. Return to your hotel by dusk & overnight.

  - label: Day 8
    title: Padum – Chilling (135 km/4 to 5 hrs, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, drive north toward Zangla village and explore the hilltop ruins of Zangla Palace and the small Zangla Chumo Nunnery. The Zangla Palace is the ancestral home of the Kings of Zangla. It is historically famous as the place where the Hungarian scholar Alexander Csoma de Kőrös stayed in 1823 to compile the first English-Tibetan dictionary. Proceed along the recently built, spectacular engineering marvel—the Nimmu-Padum-Darcha road—which cuts through deep Zanskar river gorges to reach Chilling. Overnight in Chilling (or a local homestay nearby).

  - label: Day 9
    title: Chilling – Leh (Zanskar River Rafting) (50 km/1.5 hrs, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, go to explore the Chilling – the absolute hub for white-water rafting in Ladakh. Participate in a thrilling rafting expedition down the freezing, emerald waters of the Zanskar River, ending near the spectacular Sangam (the confluence where the Zanskar meets the Indus River at Nimmu). In the afternoon, drive back toward Leh. On the way, stop at the Magnetic Hill and the army-run Gurudwara Pathar Sahib. Overnight in Leh.

goodtoknow: |-
  -Ladakh’s dramatic landscapes, monasteries and high-altitude villages offer endless opportunities for striking photography.
  -Carry spare batteries and memory cards—the cold and remote terrain can make charging and backups challenging.
  -Acclimatise gradually to Ladakh’s high altitude and keep the first few days relaxed.
  -Early mornings and late afternoons offer the best light for capturing Ladakh’s mountains, monasteries and valleys.
  -Weather changes quickly in Ladakh; carry layers and protect your camera from dust, cold and sudden temperature changes.
  -From ancient monasteries to rugged mountains, Ladakh rewards patience, observation and a keen photographic eye.
