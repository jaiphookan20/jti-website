  - label: 'Day 2: Guwahati'
    title: Guwahati sightseeing
    body: After breakfast proceed to Sualkuchi, a small town on the bank of the Brahmaputra River, known widely for its silk weaving. Upon reaching Sualkuchi, visit its silk weaving workshops to see the whole process of silk manufacturing, from cocoon to spinning, dyeing, and finally to hand weaving into exquisite saris and dress lengths. Later proceed to Amchong Tea Estate – situated just 25 km away from Guwahati city. On arrival, take a tour of the tea garden and learn how to grow and process tea, including tea tasting activity. Return to your hotel and overnight. Sualkuchi lies on the northern bank of the Brahmaputra River, also referred to as the Manchester of Assam. This little town is renowned worldwide for the production of high-quality Muga, Pat, and Eri silk products, especially Muga silk or Golden silk which is endemic to Assam. Local attires like ‘Mekhela Chador’, ‘Sari’, and ‘Gamosa’ are made from these indigenous silk materials, which have a great demand throughout Assam and India. This village has around 30,000 functional weaving looms and holds the record of producing nearly six million meters of Muga and Pat silk annually.

  - label: Day 3
    title: Guwahati – Nameri National Park (210 km/4.5 hrs. approx.)
    body: 'After breakfast, visit the famous Kamakhya temple, situated on top of Nilachal hill. This temple is revered as one of the most sacred and ancient 51 Shakti Peethas in the Indian subcontinent and is devoted to the eternal feminine, the goddess of desire, “Kamakhya” – granter of desires. It symbolizes the "fusion of faiths and practices" of Aryan and non-Aryan elements in Assam. Later you will begin your journey to Nameri National Park. Check in at the hotel on arrival. Later you can go for a short walk in the nearby areas of your hotel. Overnight. *Kamakhya temple remains open: 08:00 AM – 01:00 PM & 02.30 PM – 05.15 PM Nameri is a scenic national park situated at the foothills of the Eastern Himalayas on the Assam-Arunachal border. It touches the Pakke Tiger reserve in the south-east boundary. The park has a great variety of mammals like elephants, tigers, Indian gaur, 4 species of deer, capped langur etc. This park is also known for its birdlife. Nameri is the home of the elusive and endangered white-winged wood duck, Ibis bill, Pied Kingfisher and Great Indian Hornbill. Among the other parks, Nameri has the highest number of this species in the world along with more than 350 species of birds. The advantage of this wonderful park is that visitors have to do explore the park on foot, as there is no jeep able roads there. Rafting is another option where expert boatmen will steer your rafts while you are free to look for the aquatic birds.'

  - label: Day 5
    title: ''
    body: Nameri National Park – Kaziranga National Park (90 km / 2 hrs, approx.) Today we shall go to Kaziranga National Park. On the way, we will explore the historic city of Tezpur and visit Agnigarh, Chitralekha Udyan (Cole park) & 6th century Da Parbatia temple ruins with its beautifully carved portal. Check in at the hotel on arrival. Later go and visit Kaziranga Orchid & Bio Diversity Park. It has an Orchidarium where you can witness varieties of orchids and other plant species with medicinal value. This park also has an in-house museum displaying art, handicraft items and local musical instruments that represent the culture and heritage of the region. There is also a cultural arena that hosts dance performances of different ethnic communities of Assam. Return to hotel and overnight. Applauded for its greater One-horned Rhinoceros conservation efforts, Kaziranga National Park is located in the Golaghat and Nagaon districts of Assam. The popular national park is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site and houses two-thirds of the total world population of the greater One-horned Rhinoceros. Bordered by the Brahmaputra River to the north and the Karbi (Mikir) Hills to the south, Kaziranga National Park sprawls in a total area of 1,090 sq. km, having a core area of 430 sq. km. Apart from the One-horned Rhinoceros, it also boasts a high density of Tigers and a large breeding ground for Elephants, Swamp Deer and Wild Water Buffaloes. The national park is also recognized as an Important Bird Area (IBA) making it not only an ideal wildlife sighting destination in India but a birdwatcher’s paradise as well.

  - label: Day 9
    title: Kohima
    body: Today we shall walk to Kisama Heritage Grounds, the main area where most of the festival activities take place. Full day at Hornbill Festival. Return to your guest house & overnight. Hornbill Festival is celebrated for the first ten days of December (1st to 10th December) every year within the confines of a heritage village Kisama, Kohima, Hornbill Festival is an annual cultural extravaganza celebrated by the tribal people in the state of Nagaland. Named after the hornbill, a globally respected bird that shows up in the folklore of most of the state’s tribes. This Festival aims at sustaining, reviving, and protecting the richness of the Naga culture and heritage. This also gives visitors, an opportunity to get a ringside view of the enchanting land, its people and its culture which is done through a cultural extravaganza worth experiencing. This ten-day-long festival unites all of Nagaland, in one giant colour-splashed hodgepodge of dances, folk songs, handlooms, handicrafts, parades, games, sports, ethnic food fairs and religious ceremonies of all Naga tribes.

    body: Kohima – Day trip to Khonoma & local sightseeing (30 km /1 hr, – one way, approx.) After breakfast, drive to Khonoma village, inhabited by the Angami tribe. This village is surrounded by lush green hills and terraced fields; it is renowned for its conservation efforts. Walk around the village, see and experience the lifestyle of villagers compared to city dwellers. Visit the 03 forts from where the Angami’s carried out attacks against British troops. Afternoon, visit the Kohima State Museum, showcasing the indigenous tribal cultures, history, and traditions of Nagaland. Then visit the famous Kohima Cathedral, built during the mid-20th century. This place has as much cultural value as to history. Also visit the Kohima War Cemetery dedicated to the soldiers who fought during the battle of Kohima in WWII. Return to the guest house and overnight. Angami people are popular for their art work and mastery in bamboo, wood crafts. With its charming traditional houses, friendly locals, and thriving biodiversity, Khonoma offers a unique experience of Naga heritage and sustainable living. Khonoma village is renowned for its valour and courage. The Khonoma gate is the symbol of valor and courage and tells the story of British infiltration into Naga Hills and how British had to face fierce retaliation from the Nagas. The Angami Naga is known to be the last bastion of the Naga warriors and there are a number of memorial stones that celebrate their achievements.

    body: 'Jorhat – Day trip to Majuli Island (1 hr drive + 1 hr, ferry – one way, approx.) After breakfast, head to Majuli Island according to the ferry schedule. Visit the Uttar Kamalabari Satra (Vaishnavite monastery) where you can interact with the monks and spend some time with them to know more about the ancient traditions. The resident monks shall perform a traditional dance performance known as ‘Gayan Bayan’ accompanied by drummers, cymbals etc. Visit the Auniati Satra, founded in the middle of the 17th century and the first one that was patronized by the Ahom kings. The satra also has an in-house museum that has a collection of well-preserved ancient manuscripts and artifacts dating back to the period of the Ahom dynasty and related to neo-Vaishnavism. Proceed to visit Samaguri Satra (Vaishnavite Monastery) known for its ancient mask making tradition, which they wear during performing ‘Bhaona’ – a traditional form of dance drama, based on various Hindu mythological stories with a religious message. Time permitting, take a stroll in a nearby village inhabited by Mishing tribe to experience the lifestyle of the villagers to learn more about their unique ways of life. Later, take a ferry back to Jorhat. Overnight. Note: - To and Fro Majuli Island, Govt. The villagers may not be seen in their traditional dresses which are normally worn on special occasions only. Majuli Island is the largest inhabited river island in the world and is a microcosm of Assamese culture. Majuli represents the rural idyll in Assam. A stronghold of the peaceful religion of neo-Vaishnavism, this elegant island is a home of several ‘Satras’ (Vaishnavite Monasteries). The great 15th-century Assamese reformer and saint, Sankardeva had set up these ‘Satras’ to make the people motivated towards culture and religion. This satras showcase a unique way of life where the faithful live simply, offering worship through gayan-bayan (songs and musical instruments) and readings.'

  - label: Day 13
    title: Jorhat – Gibbon WLS – Mon (160 km / 5.5 hrs, approx.)
    body: Early morning with packed breakfast, we shall drive to Gibbon Wildlife Sanctuary and go for a jungle walk inside the sanctuary to look out for the Hoolock Gibbon (the only ape found in India), stamped tail macaque, pig-tailed macaque, Assamese macaque, Capped Langur etc. You will be accompanied by a guard/spotter who shall assist to track and see any possible wildlife found here. Later we shall go begin our journey to Mon in Nagaland, located at an elevation of about 650 m above sea level. This district is inhabited by the “Konyak” tribe. Enroute we shall organize comfort breaks. On arrival, check in at a basic but clean Guest House, owned by the local people of Mon. Overnight. ‘Konyak’ are seen by everyone as the most traditional of the sixteen Naga tribes. They had been the head hunters and collectors of enemy skulls. The core people of the tribe still have brass skull necklaces at home that are symbols showing that they have taken these heads during the battle. The basic belief behind this was that headhunting can boost crop fertility. However, the rise of Christianity in the region completely stopped this tradition of headhunting in the 1960s.Though they have given up their head-hunting ways, but still live in a very traditional lifestyle. Konyaks are adept artisans and skilled craftsmen; here you can find excellent woodcarving, doas (machetes), guns, gunpowder, head brushes necklace etc. One can still find the tattooed head hunters while travel around Konyak villages.

    title: Mon – Day trip to Longwa (42 km/2 hrs, – one way, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, we will head towards the Longwa village, which is on the border ridge and offers great panoramic views of the hills not only in Indian but on the Myanmar side as well. The unique thing about the Longwa is the inhabitants of the village have dual citizenship – one of India and the other one is Myanmar. The villagers don’t need visas to move around freely. Even some families have their kitchen in Myanmar and their bedroom in India. Another interesting fact about the village is the Indo-Myanmar border passes through the house of the village king (Angh). The Angh commands about 50 villages from both sides (India-Myanmar). This Konyak village practices the true traditions and the people are still not touched by modern ways. Enjoy the scenic beauty, meet village folk and experience their culture, traditions and way of life. Return to your hotel by dusk & overnight.
