  - label: Day 2
    title: Guwahati – Sualkuchi – Wild Mahseer (220 kms/ 5 hrs.)
    body: After breakfast, drive to the little town of Sualkuchi, loud with the clatter of looms, and visit on foot its silk weaving workshops. We shall see the whole process of silk manufacture, from cocoon to spinning to dyeing and finally to hand weaving into exquisite saris and dress lengths. Later drive to Wild Mahseer, a heritage property in Balipara.  Dinner and overnight. Located on the north bank of the Brahmaputra River, Sualkuchi is the largest silk-weaving center in Assam and Northeast India. Visitors here can witness the entire manufacturing process, from cocoon production and spinning to and hand-weaving traditional Assamese attire Mekhela chadors. The town specializes in three distinct varieties of silk—Eri, Pat, and Muga—and also produces Tasar silk.

In addition to the rhinoceros, the park boasts a high density of tigers and serves as a major breeding ground for elephants, swamp deer, and wild water buffaloes. Kaziranga is also recognized as an Important Bird Area (IBA), making it both a premier wildlife destination and a paradise for birdwatchers.

On arrival in Jorhat drive to visit Sibsagar, the former capital of the Ahom kingdom, stands as a testament to an enduring legacy and a unique blend of Indian and Southeast Asian influences. Originally of Shan origin before converting to Hinduism, the Ahoms ruled Assam for six centuries until the mid-1820s. The town derives its name from the significant, man-made ‘Sivasagar’ tank and the temple located on its banks. goodtoknow: |-
  -Assam is the only place in the world producing all three major silks—Muga, Eri, and Pat.
  -Muga silk, exclusive to Assam, is prized for its natural golden sheen and exceptional durability.
  -Eri silk is known as 'Ahimsa Silk' as it is produced without killing the silkworm.
  -Nearly every Assamese household traditionally has a handloom, making weaving a way of life.
  -Sualkuchi, Assam's 'Silk Village,' is India's largest weaving hub for Muga and Pat silk.
  -Traditional Assamese motifs are inspired by nature, folklore, and local tribal heritage.
  -Many weaving techniques in Assam have been passed down through generations for centuries.
  -Handwoven Assamese textiles are celebrated for their craftsmanship, elegance, and cultural significance.
