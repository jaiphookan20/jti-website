  - label: Day 2
    title: Leh local monasteries (110 km/4 to 5 hrs, approx.)
    body: Morning drive 45 km south to Hemis Monastery, the wealthiest and largest monastery in Ladakh. Hemis (Drukpa Lineage) houses a spectacular museum of ancient thangkas, gold statues, and sacred relics. It is famous for the Hemis Festival. Mid-day, on the way back, visit Stakna Monastery, perched dramatically on a tiger-nose-shaped rock in the middle of the Indus River. Stakna (Drukpa Lineage) offers a 360-degree panoramic view of the Indus Valley and houses a beautiful silver-gilded chorten. Afternoon drive to the north-west of Leh to visit Phyang Monastery. Phyang (Drikung Kagyu Lineage) is famous for its massive 900-year-old museum containing outdoor shrines, old Kashmiri bronzes, and Chinese silks. Evening is free for leisure. Overnight in Leh.

  - label: Day 3
    title: Leh – Alchi via Basgo, Likir & Saspol Cave (75 km/3 hrs, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, drive down the Srinagar-Leh highway. Stop at Basgo Monastery, a crumbling mud-brick fortress complex overlooking the village. The Basgo Fort is listed among the World Monuments Watch; it represents the old capital of lower Ladakh. The temples house a massive clay idol of Maitreya (Future Buddha). Continue to Likir Monastery, situated a short detour from the highway. Likir (Gelugpa Lineage) is renowned for its colossal 75-foot outdoor golden statue of Maitreya Buddha. Drive to Saspol Caves, located just off the road near the bridge. Saspol Caves are rare rock-cut cave temples dating back to the 13th-century, featuring stunning Buddhist wall paintings that fuse Tibetan and Indian art forms. Cross the river to reach the quiet village of Alchi. Overnight.

  - label: Day 4
    title: Alchi – Lamayuru (60 km/2 hrs, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, spend the morning exploring the Alchi Monastery Complex and enjoying a peaceful walk through the surrounding apricot orchards and traditional Ladakhi homes. Unlike other monasteries built on hills, Alchi sits on flat ground. It is one of the oldest preserved complexes in Ladakh (11th century). Its murals are global masterpieces, preserving a vibrant Kashmiri style of Buddhist art that has vanished elsewhere. In the afternoon, drive along the Indus River, ascending through the Khangral gorge towards Lamayuru. Overnight.

  - label: Day 5
    title: Lamayuru – Mulbekh & Kargil (105 km/4 hrs, approx.)
    body: After breakfast, explore Lamayuru Monastery and look down at the "Moonland" landscape. Perched precariously on a surreal, eroded clay landform resembling the moon. It is shrouded in myth as a place where a holy man dried up a deep lake. Mid-day, drive across the Fotu La Pass (the highest point on the Srinagar-Leh highway at 13,478 ft) down to Mulbekh to witness the ancient rock sculpture. Mulbekh Chamba is a magnificent, 9-meter-high rock-carved relief of Maitreya Buddha dating back to the Kushan or early medieval era (approx. 7th-8th century), standing right beside the highway. Later drive into Kargil town. Check-in at the hotel & overnight.

  - label: Day 6
    title: Kargil – Rangdum via Suru Valley (130 km/4 ½ to 5 ½ hrs, approx.)
    body: 'Altitude: 3,650 meters (11,975 feet) at Rangdum After breakfast, drive into the lush Suru Valley, passing through traditional riverside villages and fields of green barley. Mid-day, enjoy viewpoints of the colossal Nun Kun peaks that provides unobstructed, breathtaking views of Mount Nun (7,135 m) and Mount Kun (7,077 m), the highest twin peaks in the Zanskar range. Take a short walk near the foot of the Parkachik Glacier – a massive wall of tumbling glacial ice that rolls directly down to meet the rushing waters of the Suru River. Afternoon, arrive in the isolated, windswept plains of Rangdum and visit its fortress-like monastery. Rangdum (Gelugpa Lineage) is an 18th-century monastery sitting solitary on a central hillock, acting like an ancient sentinel guarding a desolate valley. Overnight at Rangdum (Basic homestay or camps).'

  - label: Day 7
    title: ''
    body: 'Rangdum – Padum via Penzila Top & Drang-Drung Glacier (105 km/4 to 5 hrs, approx.) Altitude: 3,600 meters (11,810 feet) at Padum After breakfast, drive past the plains and begin the ascent up to the scenic Penzila mountain pass. The Penzila Top is a high mountain pass at 14,435 feet that serves as the breathtaking physical gateway separating the Suru and Zanskar Valleys. Mid-day, stop at the mountain summit to look down at the sweeping Drang-Drung glacier – a massive, winding river of ice that looks like a frozen highway. It is the largest glacier in the region outside of Siachen. In the afternoon, descend into the core Zanskar Valley, driving alongside the Stod River until reaching Padum. Overnight in Padum.'

  - label: Day 8
    title: Padum local sightseeing (40 km/2 hrs approx.)
    body: 'Altitude: 3,600 meters (11,810 feet) around the valley floor After breakfast, visit Sani Gompa, located flat on the valley floor. It is believed to be the oldest monastery in Zanskar, belonging to the Drukpa line. This holds a sacred grove and an ancient Kanika stupa that dates back to the Indian Emperor Kanishka (2nd century AD). Drive 14 km out of Padum along the Stod River to visit the Tungri Nunnery (Samten Choling) – a rare, offbeat experience. It houses a small, self-sustaining community of Buddhist nuns (Chumos). The lifestyle here is simple, offering profound insights into female monasticism in the Himalayas Cross the valley to visit Karsha Monastery, the largest and grandest monastery in Zanskar. Karsha (Gelugpa Lineage) is aspectacular, sprawling white monastic complex tiered across a steep mountain cliff. It houses ancient avalokiteshvara shrines and offers panoramic views of the Padum valley floor. Return to your hotel by dusk & overnight.'

    title: ''
    body: 'Padum – Day hike to Phugtal Cave Monastery (2.5 hrs drive each way to the trailhead + 3 to 4 hrs of round-trip hiking, approx.) Altitude: 3,850 meters (12,630 feet) at Phugtal After breakfast, drive from Padum deep into the Lungnak Valley to the road terminus near Cha or Purne village. Phugtal (Gelugpa Lineage) is a spectacular cliffside monastery built directly into the mouth of a massive limestone cave. Its structure resembles a giant clay honeycomb hanging over a river gorge. Later set off on a dramatic cliffside trek following the striking, turquoise waters of the Tsarap River. In the afternoon, reach the isolated cave monastery, have lunch, explore the cliffs, and hike back to your vehicle for the return drive. Overnight at Padum.'

    title: ''
    body: 'Padum – Chilling via Zangla Palace & Chumo Nunnery (125 km/4 hrs, approx.) Altitude: 3,200 meters (10,500 feet) at Chilling After breakfast, drive north towards Zangla village and explore the hilltop ruins of Zangla Palace and the small Zangla Chumo Nunnery. The Zangla Palace is the ancestral home of the Kings of Zangla. It is historically famous as the place where the Hungarian scholar Alexander Csoma de Kőrös stayed in 1823 to compile the first English-Tibetan dictionary. Proceed along the recently built, spectacular engineering marvel—the Nimmu-Padum-Darcha road—which cuts through deep Zanskar river gorges to reach Chilling. Overnight in Chilling (or a local homestay nearby).'

  - label: Day 11
    title: Chilling – Leh (Zanskar River Rafting) (50 km/1.5 hrs, approx.)
    body: 'Altitude: 3,500 meters (11,500 feet) at Leh After breakfast, go to explore the Chilling – the absolute hub for white-water rafting in Ladakh. Gear up for a thrilling white-water rafting trip down the freezing, emerald waters of the Zanskar River through the grand Zanskar canyon, ending near the spectacular Sangam (the confluence where the Zanskar meets the Indus River at Nimmu). In the afternoon, drive back toward Leh. On the way, stop at the Magnetic Hill and the army-run Gurudwara Pathar Sahib. Spend your final evening shopping for souvenirs in Leh Market. Overnight in Leh. Note: Zanskar Rafting is an exhilarating run navigating Grade III/IV rapids inside deep stone gorges, ending where the green Zanskar and muddy Indus rivers merge at Sangam.'
