There are wildlife sightings that excite you, and then there are sightings that leave you wondering whether you really saw what you think you saw.

Somewhere among the tall grass, woodland and riverine forests of Manas National Park, a dark shape can appear almost out of nowhere. It moves silently, disappears behind the vegetation, and leaves behind only a fleeting impression of deep black fur and two watchful eyes.

The legendary **black panther of Manas** is one such mystery.

In recent years, rare sightings and photographs of a melanistic leopard in Manas have brought renewed attention to this elusive animal. In July 2026, the Chief Minister's Office of Assam shared visuals of a melanistic black panther from Manas, describing it as the "Ghost Rider at Manas."

But what exactly is a black panther? And why is seeing one in the wild such a remarkable experience?

## A Black Panther Is Actually a Leopard

Perhaps the first thing to understand is that a black panther is **not a separate species**.

In India, the term generally refers to a **melanistic leopard** (_Panthera pardus_). Melanism is a genetic condition that causes an unusually high concentration of the dark pigment melanin in the animal's coat.

Under the right light, however, the leopard's familiar rosette markings can still be seen beneath the dark coat.

So, while a normal leopard may be recognised by its golden coat and black rosettes, its melanistic counterpart appears almost entirely black.

This unusual appearance is what has given the animal its mysterious reputation.

## Why Is It Called a "Panther"?

The word _panther_ can be confusing.

It is not the scientific name of a separate big-cat species. In common usage, "black panther" can refer to a melanistic leopard in Asia and Africa, or a melanistic jaguar in the Americas.

In the forests of Northeast India, therefore, the black panther people speak of is essentially a **leopard with a very dark coat**.

And that coat can be remarkably effective camouflage.

## A Shadow Among the Grass

Imagine travelling quietly through Manas at first light.

The forest is beginning to wake. Birds call from the trees, golden grass moves gently in the breeze, and somewhere in the distance an elephant disappears into the woodland.

Then the guide suddenly slows the vehicle.

Everyone looks ahead.

For a few seconds, there is nothing.

Then something moves.

A dark silhouette crosses an opening between the grass and forest. It pauses for a moment before slipping back into the shadows.

Was it a leopard?

Perhaps.

And perhaps, if you were extraordinarily fortunate, it was the elusive melanistic one.

This is what makes a black-panther sighting so special. Unlike animals that are relatively easy to locate, a melanistic leopard can blend remarkably well into the darker sections of the forest.

A sighting may last only seconds.

Sometimes, that is all the forest gives you.

## Why Manas Is So Special

Located along the foothills of the Eastern Himalayas in western Assam, Manas National Park is one of India's great wildlife landscapes.

The park was originally established as a game reserve in 1928, became a Tiger Reserve in 1974, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985, a Biosphere Reserve in 1989, and a National Park in 1990. Today, it protects an extraordinary mixture of grasslands, forests and riverine habitats.

Its biodiversity is exceptional.

Alongside the Bengal tiger and leopard, Manas supports clouded leopards, fishing cats, jungle cats, leopard cats and the elusive Asiatic golden cat. The park is also home to elephants, Indian one-horned rhinoceros, wild water buffalo, swamp deer, pygmy hogs and the endangered golden langur, among many other species.

More than 500 species of birds have also been recorded in the wider Manas landscape, making the park as rewarding for birdwatchers as it is for mammal enthusiasts.

The black panther is therefore only one piece of a much larger ecological story.

## More Than a Rare Photograph

It is tempting to think of a black panther simply as a prize sighting — something to tick off a wildlife enthusiast's list.

But its presence tells us something much more important.

A leopard needs a functioning ecosystem. It needs prey, cover, water and sufficiently large areas of habitat in which to hunt and move.

When an elusive predator survives in a landscape as diverse as Manas, it is part of a much larger network of life.

Protecting the leopard therefore means protecting the forest, grasslands, prey species and ecological corridors that support it.

This is one reason rare sightings can generate so much excitement among conservationists and wildlife enthusiasts.

## The Mystery of Manas

Manas has always had a certain sense of mystery.

Perhaps it comes from its location at the meeting point of the Himalayan foothills and the plains of Assam. Perhaps it comes from its rivers, tall grasslands and dense forests. Or perhaps it is simply because the park still has the ability to surprise even experienced wildlife observers.

You may enter the park hoping to see a tiger and encounter a rhino.

You may search for a rhino and find a herd of wild buffalo instead.

You may spend hours watching the grasslands and suddenly see a golden langur moving through the trees.

And occasionally, the forest may offer something even rarer — a dark figure moving silently through the grass.

The black panther does not announce its arrival.

It does not pose for the camera.

It does not wait for the perfect photograph.

It simply appears, for a moment, and then becomes part of the forest again.

## A Reminder to Keep Looking

Wildlife tourism is often about patience.

A safari is not a zoo visit, and the forest does not operate according to a timetable. There are no guarantees.

That uncertainty is precisely what makes a genuine wildlife encounter so powerful.

The possibility of seeing a black panther should never be the sole reason to visit Manas. Instead, it should be one of the many possibilities that make the park so fascinating.

Because while you are looking for the shadow in the grass, you are also experiencing an ecosystem filled with extraordinary life.

And perhaps that is the real magic of Manas.

**The rarest animals are not always the reason to enter the forest. Sometimes, they are the reason to slow down, observe carefully, and appreciate everything around them.**

The next time a guide quietly says, _"Look there,"_ among the grass and shadows of Manas, look carefully.

You may just be looking at one of India's most elusive big cats.
